# Products

A product is the thing you sell. It carries the name, the description, the images and the rules about how
it is delivered — but not the price. Prices live on
[tiers](/plugins/ecommerce-plugin/tiers-and-prices), which is the next chapter.

# Categories

**Admin Panel → Ecommerce → Categories**

Categories group products on the storefront. Customers see them as filter buttons above the catalog.

<table>
    <thead>

    </thead>
    <tbody>
        <tr>

            <td>The filter button's label</td>
        </tr>
        <tr>
            <td><strong>Slug</strong></td>
            <td>The part that ends up in the URL (<code>/store?category=merch</code>)</td>
        </tr>
        <tr>
            <td><strong>Description</strong></td>
            <td>Shown above the products when a customer picks this category</td>
        </tr>
    </tbody>

Categories can be reordered by dragging them in the list; that order is the order customers see. A product
can be in several categories, or in none — a product with no category still shows up under "All".

Only categories that contain at least one published product appear on the storefront, so an empty category
is never a dead end.

## About the key

The key is how integrations and external systems recognise your product, independently of its name or its
URL. It never changes on its own, and it is **locked as soon as the product has been sold**.

Use lowercase letters, numbers, dashes, underscores, dots and slashes. `supporter`, `merch-hoodie` and
`acme/premium-plugin` are all fine.

<Alert severity="info">
    If you do not have integrations, the key never matters to you — but pick something readable anyway,
    because you will see it in order exports and you cannot change it after the first sale.
</Alert>

## Status

<table>
    <thead>
        <tr>
            <th>Status</th>
            <th>Effect</th>
        </tr>
    </thead>
    <tbody>
        <tr>
            <td><strong>Draft</strong></td>
            <td>Invisible in the store. Its page returns a 404 to customers. Use it while you set the product up</td>
        </tr>
        <tr>
            <td><strong>Published</strong></td>
            <td>For sale</td>
        </tr>
        <tr>
            <td><strong>Archived</strong></td>
            <td>No longer for sale, but everything about it is kept: order history, existing subscriptions, and the customers who already bought it</td>
        </tr>
    </tbody>
</table>

**Archive rather than delete.** A product that has been sold can no longer be deleted at all — the store
refuses and points you at archiving — because deleting it would tear a hole in your order history.

Archiving does **not** cancel existing subscriptions. They keep renewing until you or the customer cancel
them. If you want a product gone completely, archive it and then cancel its subscriptions from
**Ecommerce → Subscriptions**.

# Images

## The cover image

Set on the product form. It is what customers see on the catalog tile, and the first image on the product
page. It is also the image used when someone shares a link to your product on Discord, X or Facebook, so
give it a decent 1200×630-ish crop if you care about that.

## The gallery

The **Gallery** tab holds extra images, shown after the cover image on the product page. Upload several at
once, drag the rows to reorder them, and delete a row to remove the file.

![Product gallery](/user-manual/plugins/ecommerce-plugin/product-gallery.png)

Each image can be up to 4 MB.

<Alert severity="info">
    Images are resized automatically for the tile, the product page, the thumbnails and the social preview.
    Upload the best quality you have and let the store scale it down.
</Alert>

# What customers see

![A product page](/user-manual/plugins/ecommerce-plugin/product-page.png)

The product page shows the gallery, your description, and the tiers side by side with their prices. If the
product has only one tier, its buy button sits right at the top of the page instead.

Everything is also readable without JavaScript, and product pages carry proper titles, descriptions and
preview images for search engines and link previews.

# Writing a product that sells

A few things the layout rewards:

- **Short description**: one line, concrete. It is all the catalog tile shows.
- **Description**: the full pitch. Headings and lists are rendered properly.
- **Tier description**: use a **bulleted list**. Bullets are rendered as a feature list with check marks, so
  a tier's list of perks lines up neatly against the other tiers.
- **One product, one thing.** If two variants are delivered differently — one shipped, one instant — they
  are two products, not two tiers.
