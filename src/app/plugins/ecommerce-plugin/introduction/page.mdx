# Introduction

The Ecommerce plugin turns your forumify community into a shop. You can sell one-off products and
recurring subscriptions, take payments through Stripe, and hand out roles and badges to the people who buy
them — all without leaving your admin panel.

It is built for the things communities actually sell:

- **Donations and supporter ranks** — a monthly subscription that grants a "Supporter" role and badge, and
  takes them back when the subscription ends.
- **Digital goods** — in-game items, server perks, or downloads that an admin hands over by hand.
- **Merchandise** — physical goods with a shipping address collected at checkout.
- **Services and licenses** — anything delivered by an integration after payment.

# Installation

Package name for manual installation: `forumify/forumify-ecommerce-plugin`

Once the plugin is installed and activated, an **Ecommerce** section appears in your admin panel.

# How it fits together

Three ideas carry through the whole plugin. Getting them straight now makes everything else obvious.

**A product has tiers, and a tier has prices.**

```
Product  "Supporter"
  ├─ Tier  "Bronze"  ─┬─ Price  €3 / month
  │                   └─ Price  €30 / year
  └─ Tier  "Gold"    ─┬─ Price  €10 / month
                      └─ Price  €100 / year
```

A **product** is the thing you sell. A **tier** is a variant of it — Bronze and Gold, or Free and Premium.
A **price** is one way to pay for a tier. Customers pick a tier, then pick how they want to be billed.

**An order is one purchase of one price.** There is no shopping cart: each order is a single product, at a
single price, paid in one go. A subscription produces a new order every time it renews, so the order list
is also your payment history.

**Paid is not the same as completed.** *Paid* means the money arrived. *Completed* means the customer has
what they bought. For an instant download those happen at the same moment; for a hoodie you have to ship,
the order sits in between and waits for you.

<Alert severity="info">
    Everything that happens to money is reported by your payment provider, not decided by the store.
    When Stripe says a payment succeeded, failed, renewed or was refunded, the store follows. That is why
    setting up the webhook correctly matters more than any other single step.
</Alert>

# What you need

- A **payment provider account**. The plugin ships with Stripe support, which covers cards, wallets and
  most local payment methods.
- A **site reachable over HTTPS**, so your provider can send webhooks to it.
- **Background workers running** on your site (the message consumer and the scheduler). They send email
  notifications and handle subscriptions that fall behind on payment.
