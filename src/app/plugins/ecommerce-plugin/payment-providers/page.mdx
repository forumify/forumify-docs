# Payment Providers

A payment provider is the service that actually takes your customers' money. The store never handles card
details itself: customers are sent to the provider's own checkout, pay there, and the provider tells your
store what happened.

The plugin ships with **Stripe**. Other providers can be added by plugins, and everything in this chapter
applies to them too.

# The provider list

**Admin Panel → Ecommerce → Payment providers**

![Payment providers](/user-manual/plugins/ecommerce-plugin/payment-providers.png)

Each provider shows two things:

- **Enabled / Disabled** — whether it is offered at checkout.
- **Configured / Not configured** — whether it has the credentials it needs.

A provider is only offered to customers when it is **both enabled and configured**.

<Alert severity="info">
    Disabling a provider stops <em>new</em> checkouts. Payments already in flight, renewals of existing
    subscriptions and refunds keep working, so turning one off never strands a customer mid-purchase.
</Alert>

If you have several providers enabled, customers choose between them on the checkout page. With exactly one,
the choice is skipped.

# Setting up Stripe

You need a [Stripe account](https://dashboard.stripe.com). Stripe is available in most countries and handles
cards, wallets like Apple Pay and Google Pay, and a range of local payment methods — which ones your
customers see is configured in your Stripe dashboard, not here.

## Step 1: get your secret key

In the Stripe dashboard, go to **Developers → API keys** and copy your **secret key**.

- A test-mode key starts with `sk_test_` (or `rk_test_` for a restricted key).
- A live-mode key starts with `sk_live_` (or `rk_live_`).

If you prefer a **restricted key**, it needs write access to Checkout Sessions, Customers, Products, Prices
and Subscriptions.

Paste it into the **Secret key** field on the Stripe page in your admin panel.

![Stripe settings](/user-manual/plugins/ecommerce-plugin/stripe-settings.png)

<Alert severity="info">
    Saved keys are never shown back to you. The field shows a masked hint of what is stored, and leaving it
    empty when you edit the provider keeps the existing key.
</Alert>

## Step 2: add the webhook

This is the step that makes the store work. A **webhook** is how Stripe tells your store that a payment
succeeded, a subscription renewed, or a card was declined. Without it, your customers pay and your store
never finds out.

The Stripe page in your admin panel shows the **webhook URL** for your site. It looks like:

```
https://your-community.com/store/webhook/stripe
```

In the Stripe dashboard, go to **Developers → Webhooks → Add endpoint**, paste that URL, and select all
events from these four groups:

- **checkout**
- **invoice**
- **customer**
- **charge**

Stripe's event picker lets you tick a whole group at once, so this is four clicks.

<Alert severity="info">
    Those groups contain more events than the store acts on, and that is fine: anything it does not
    recognise is simply acknowledged and ignored. Picking whole groups means you cannot accidentally leave
    out the one event that matters — a missing <code>invoice.paid</code>, for instance, would stop
    subscriptions renewing, and you would not find out until one failed to renew.
</Alert>

## Step 3: paste the signing secret

After creating the endpoint, Stripe shows its **signing secret**, starting with `whsec_`. Copy it into the
**Webhook signing secret** field and save.

The store uses this secret to verify that incoming webhooks really came from Stripe. A request with a bad
signature is rejected.

## Step 4: enable it

Tick **Enabled** and save. Stripe now shows as *Enabled* and *Configured*, and appears at checkout.

# Test mode first

Stripe's test mode is a complete copy of Stripe that uses fake money. Use it to run real purchases through
your store before you take a cent from anyone.

1. Enter your **test** secret key and create a webhook endpoint in test mode.
2. Buy your own product with a [test card](https://docs.stripe.com/testing) — `4242 4242 4242 4242`, any
   future expiry date, any CVC.
3. Watch the order reach **Completed**, check your notification email, and confirm any roles were granted.

## Switching to live keys

Replace the secret key with your live key and add a live-mode webhook endpoint with the same URL and events.

The store keeps test-mode and live-mode data apart: products, prices and customers created in test mode are
never reused with live keys. The first live purchase of a price simply creates a fresh copy of it in your
live Stripe account. Your existing orders keep their history either way.

<Alert severity="warning">
    Test-mode subscriptions do not migrate to live mode. If members subscribed during your testing, cancel
    those subscriptions before you go live so they do not linger as active subscriptions that Stripe will
    never renew.
</Alert>

# How your catalog reaches Stripe

You do not have to create anything in Stripe by hand, and you should not.

- Your store is the source of truth for prices. Editing a product never calls Stripe.
- The first time someone buys a given price, the store creates the matching Product and Price in Stripe
  automatically.
- Each **tier** becomes a Stripe Product, named "Product – Tier".
- Each **price** becomes a Stripe Price.

When you [change a price](/plugins/ecommerce-plugin/tiers-and-prices#changing-a-price), the old Stripe Price
is archived the first time the new one is used. Existing subscriptions keep billing at the old amount, which
is exactly what Stripe does too.

<Alert severity="info">
    Renaming a product or tier in your store does <strong>not</strong> rename it in Stripe. The Stripe name
    is set once, when the price is first sold. It affects what customers see on Stripe's checkout page and
    on their invoices, so if the name matters, get it right before the first sale.
</Alert>

# What customers see at checkout

- Customers must be **logged in** to check out. A guest is asked to log in or register and comes straight
  back to what they were buying.
- They are redirected to Stripe's hosted checkout page, which handles cards, wallets and whatever else you
  enabled in Stripe.
- After paying they land back on their order page, which updates itself while the payment is confirmed.
- Their email, display name and a link back to their forumify account are passed to Stripe, and a returning
  customer reuses the same Stripe customer record.

Free prices skip the provider entirely — there is nothing to charge.

# Refunds, invoices and failed payments

Stripe supports all three of the customer self-service features:

- **Refunds** — approving a refund request sends the full amount back through Stripe.
- **Invoices** — customers can download the Stripe invoice PDF from their order.
- **Fixing a failed payment** — a customer whose renewal failed gets a link to Stripe's hosted invoice page,
  where they can pay and replace their card.

A provider added by another plugin may support only some of these. The buttons appear only where the
provider behind that order can actually handle them.
