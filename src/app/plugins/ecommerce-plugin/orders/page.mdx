# Orders

An order is one purchase of one price. Subscription renewals are orders too, so the order list is the
complete record of every payment your store has ever taken.

# How a purchase happens

1. A customer picks a tier and a billing option on the product page and clicks the buy button.
2. **They log in.** Checkout always requires an account; a guest is sent to log in or register and comes back
   to exactly what they were buying.
3. The checkout page shows what they are buying, asks for anything the fulfillment type needs (a username, a
   shipping address), and lets them choose a payment provider if you have several enabled.

![The checkout page](/user-manual/plugins/ecommerce-plugin/checkout.png)

4. They confirm, and the store creates a **pending** order and sends them to the provider.
5. They pay, and come back to their order page, which updates itself while the payment is confirmed.

Free prices skip step 4 and 5 — the order is paid on the spot.

<Alert severity="info">
    There is no shopping cart. Each order is a single product at a single price. Customers who want two
    things buy them one after the other, and you get two clean orders.
</Alert>

How they move:

```
Pending ──paid──> Paid ──completed──> Completed
   │                │                     │
   ├──> Failed      └──────> Refunded <────┘
   └──> Cancelled
```

A few things worth knowing, because they show up in real stores:

- **Money arriving late always wins.** A customer who abandoned checkout and then paid anyway — or whose
  delayed bank payment landed a day later — moves from Failed or Cancelled to Paid. You do not have to fix
  those by hand.
- **A refund is final.** Nothing moves an order out of Refunded.
- **Cancelled means abandoned, not refused.** Your customer walked away from the payment page or it expired.
  Nobody was charged and nothing is wrong.

# The order list

**Admin Panel → Ecommerce → Orders**

![Order list](/user-manual/plugins/ecommerce-plugin/order-list.png)

Filter by **status** and by **product**, and search by order reference, product or customer. The two
views you will actually use:

- **Paid** — your fulfillment queue: everything paid for and not yet handed over. The **Orders (3)** count in
  the menu is exactly this.
- **All** — the full history, for looking up a customer's payment.

Renewal orders are tagged **Renewal**, so you can tell a new customer from a subscription that billed again.

# The order page

Open an order to see everything recorded about it:

![Order details](/user-manual/plugins/ecommerce-plugin/order-view.png)

- **Reference** — the public code the customer quotes when they contact you.
- **Customer**, **product**, **tier**, **price** and the dates it was ordered, paid, completed or refunded.
- **Payment provider** and its **provider reference** — the id to search for in your Stripe dashboard.
- **Fulfillment details** — the shipping address, or whatever the customer entered at checkout.
- A link to the **subscription** the order started or renewed, if there is one.
- **Mark as completed**, with an optional note for the customer.
- Any **refund request** on the order, with approve and reject.

Everything about an order is a snapshot taken at the moment of sale: the product title, the tier label, the
amount and the interval. Editing the product later, or archiving it, never changes what an old order says.

<Alert severity="info">
    That is also why orders cannot be edited or deleted. An order is a record of something that happened.
    If it needs undoing, refund it.
</Alert>

# What customers see

## My purchases

`/store/orders`. Logged-in members get a link to it at the top of the store catalog, and every
notification and order page links back to it.

![My purchases](/user-manual/plugins/ecommerce-plugin/my-purchases.png)

Two parts:

- **Their subscriptions**, as cards. Anything needing attention (a failed payment) is at the top.
- **Their order history**, as a list: date, reference, product, amount and status, newest first. Renewals are
  marked.

## An order page

`/store/order/{reference}` — the page they land on after paying, and the one every notification links to.

Depending on the order they may also get:

- **Pay now** — finish an unpaid order at the provider.
- **Download invoice** — the provider's invoice PDF.
- **Request a refund** — if the product has a refund period and the order still qualifies. See
  [Refunds](/plugins/ecommerce-plugin/refunds).

The page refreshes itself every few seconds while an order is pending, so a customer returning from their
bank sees it flip to paid without touching anything.

<Alert severity="info">
    Order pages are private: only the customer who placed the order can open theirs.
</Alert>

# Invoices

Invoices come from your payment provider, not from the store — they are the bookkeeping record, and the
provider is the one who issued them. Customers download theirs from their order page whenever the provider
behind that order supports it.

With Stripe, that is every paid order: one-time payments get an invoice generated at checkout, and
subscriptions always invoice.
