# Refunds

Customers ask, you decide. The store never refunds anything automatically: "14 days, no questions asked" is
a policy your community sets, not a rule the plugin enforces on your money.

# Making a product refundable

On the product form, set **Refund period** to the number of days after delivery that customers may ask for
their money back. Leave it empty and the product is not refundable — that is the default.

<Alert severity="info">
    The refund period is copied onto each order when it is placed. Changing it later never changes what an
    existing customer was promised, in either direction.
</Alert>

The clock starts at delivery, not at payment. An order you have not got round to completing stays refundable
until you do, which is the fair way round.

# Handling a request

A customer asks from their order page, with a reason. You get the request in two places:

- **Admin Panel → Ecommerce → Refunds**, which lists everything waiting. The menu item carries the count —
  **Refunds (1)**.
- On the order itself, where you can see the whole context.

![Refund requests](/user-manual/plugins/ecommerce-plugin/refunds.png)

On the order itself you see why they asked, and decide:

![Handling a refund request](/user-manual/plugins/ecommerce-plugin/refund-handle.png)

You have two buttons, and an optional **note for the customer** that is shown to them either way:

- **Approve and refund** — the full amount goes back through the payment provider, the order becomes
  **Refunded**, and the customer is notified.
- **Reject** — the order is left exactly as it is, and the customer is told their request was declined.

One request per order. A customer whose request is rejected cannot ask again through the store, so put the
reason in the note.

<Alert severity="warning">
    There is no notification when a refund request comes in — the count next to the <strong>Refunds</strong>
    menu item is what tells you. If you sell refundable products, get into the habit of glancing at it.
</Alert>

# What a refund undoes

Approving a refund:

- sends the **full amount** back through the provider;
- moves the order to **Refunded**, which is final;
- **revokes the roles and badges** a one-time order granted, under the usual rules — roles the member already
  had, or that another live purchase also grants, stay put;
- emails the customer.

If the order is a payment of a subscription that is still running — its first payment or a renewal —
approving it also **ends that subscription right away**. That is what takes its roles and badges back, and
it makes sure someone who got their money back is never charged again. The approve form warns you, and the
customer is told before they send the request. If the payment provider can't end the subscription, nothing
is refunded and you can try again.

The [cancel screen](/plugins/ecommerce-plugin/subscriptions#as-an-admin)'s **Also refund the last payment**
option works the same way.

# Refunds that start somewhere else

Not every refund goes through this page, and the store keeps up either way:

- **A refund you issue in the Stripe dashboard** is reported back and the order becomes Refunded here too,
  with the same consequences.
- **A lost chargeback** — a customer disputed the charge with their bank and the bank sided with them — is
  treated as a refund. A dispute you win changes nothing.

<Alert severity="info">
    <strong>Partial refunds are not supported.</strong> If you partially refund in your provider's dashboard,
    the store notices it and deliberately leaves the order alone: it is neither fully refunded nor
    untouched, and the store will not pretend otherwise. The money still moves — it is just not reflected in
    the order's status, and no roles are revoked.
</Alert>

# What customers see

On their order page, if the order qualifies:

> You can ask for a refund until 14 March 2026 — **Request a refund**

They click it, write why, and send. From then on the order page shows the state of their request:

An approved refund also sends them an email, and the money takes however long their bank takes — typically
five to ten days.

# A sensible policy

The plugin does not decide these for you, but a few defaults that work:

- **Digital goods delivered instantly**: a short window, 7 or 14 days, or none at all.
- **Physical goods**: a window long enough to cover delivery plus your local statutory cooling-off period.
- **Subscriptions**: often none. A customer who cancels keeps the period they paid for anyway, which is
  usually enough.

Whatever you choose, write it somewhere your customers can read it before they buy — a CMS page linked from
the store, for instance.
