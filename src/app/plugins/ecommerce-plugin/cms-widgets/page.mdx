# CMS Widgets

`/store` is a perfectly good shop, but it is a catalog. When you want a **landing page** — a pitch, a
pricing table, a donation call to action on your homepage — build one in the
[page builder](/user-manual/cms) with the Ecommerce widgets.

Open any CMS page in the builder and look for the **Ecommerce** group in the widget sidebar.

![Ecommerce widgets in the page builder](/user-manual/plugins/ecommerce-plugin/cms-widgets.png)

# Product

One product, rendered as the same tile the catalog uses: image, title, short description and starting
price, linking to the product page.

<table>
    <thead>
        <tr>
            <th>Setting</th>
            <th>Effect</th>
        </tr>
    </thead>
    <tbody>
        <tr>
            <td><strong>Product</strong></td>
            <td>Which product to show</td>
        </tr>
        <tr>
            <td><strong>Hide image</strong> / <strong>Hide description</strong> / <strong>Hide price</strong></td>
            <td>Drop that part of the tile</td>
        </tr>
    </tbody>
</table>

Only **published** products render. If the product is a draft, or you delete it later, the widget renders
nothing rather than linking to a page that 404s — so a page built ahead of a launch simply comes to life
when you publish the product.

# Product Category

A heading, a grid of product tiles from one category, and a **Show more** link into the filtered catalog.

<table>
    <thead>
        <tr>
            <th>Setting</th>
            <th>Effect</th>
        </tr>
    </thead>
    <tbody>
        <tr>
            <td><strong>Category</strong></td>
            <td>Which category to pull from</td>
        </tr>
        <tr>
            <td><strong>Limit</strong></td>
            <td>How many tiles to show (default 4)</td>
        </tr>
        <tr>
            <td><strong>Hide title</strong></td>
            <td>Drop the heading</td>
        </tr>
    </tbody>
</table>

The "Show more" link only appears when the category actually holds more products than you are showing.

An empty category, or one you delete, renders nothing.

# Pricing Table

The product page's tier comparison as a standalone block: every purchasable tier side by side, with billing
options and working buy buttons.

<table>
    <thead>
        <tr>
            <th>Setting</th>
            <th>Effect</th>
        </tr>
    </thead>
    <tbody>
        <tr>
            <td><strong>Product</strong></td>
            <td>Which product's tiers to show</td>
        </tr>
        <tr>
            <td><strong>Title</strong></td>
            <td>Optional heading above the tiers</td>
        </tr>
    </tbody>
</table>

This is the widget for a "Support us" or "Plans" page. It is the same card as on the product page, so the
Recommended highlight, the feature lists and the checkout all behave identically.

# Buy Button

A single button straight to checkout for one tier. Meant to sit next to a Rich Text or Box widget in a hero
you built yourself.

<table>
    <thead>
        <tr>
            <th>Setting</th>
            <th>Effect</th>
        </tr>
    </thead>
    <tbody>
        <tr>
            <td><strong>Tier</strong></td>
            <td>Which tier to sell, chosen as "Product - Tier"</td>
        </tr>
        <tr>
            <td><strong>Label</strong></td>
            <td>Button text. Defaults to "Get &lt;tier&gt;"</td>
        </tr>
        <tr>
            <td><strong>Type</strong></td>
            <td>Call to action, Primary, Outlined or Link — the same styles as the platform's Button widget</td>
        </tr>
        <tr>
            <td><strong>Show price</strong></td>
            <td>Also render the price under the button</td>
        </tr>
    </tbody>
</table>

The button uses the tier's **cheapest active price**. If you later change that price, the button follows
automatically — there is nothing to update on your page.

<Alert severity="info">
    If the tier has no active price left, the button renders <strong>disabled</strong> with "This product is
    not available right now" rather than disappearing. A landing page that quietly loses its call to action
    is much harder to notice than one showing a dead button.
</Alert>

Searching submits to `/store`, so the results are the real catalog with its filters and paging — not a
second, smaller product list on your landing page.

# Building a store landing page

A layout that works, from top to bottom:

1. A **Rich Text** or **Box** widget with your pitch, and a **Buy Button** for the option most people
   should pick.
2. A **Pricing Table** for the product, so the alternatives are one scroll away.
3. A **Product Category** widget or two for your other ranges — "Merch", "Add-ons".
4. A **Catalog Search** at the bottom for everything else.

Then point your menu at that page instead of at `/store`, and keep `/store` as the full catalog behind the
search and the "Show more" links.

<Alert severity="info">
    Widget <strong>previews in the page builder</strong> are approximations — they are drawn with the admin
    panel's styles, not your theme's. Always check the real page on the frontend before you publish.
</Alert>
