# Subscriptions

A recurring price creates a subscription. Your payment provider charges the customer every interval, and the
store mirrors what the provider reports: a renewal, a failed payment, a cancellation.

Each renewal is recorded as a new **order**, so a subscription's payment history is just its list of orders.

# The lifecycle

```
Active ⇄ Past due
   │        │
   └──> Cancelled ──> Ended
```

**Ended is final.** Nothing brings an ended subscription back — a customer who wants to return subscribes
again, which is a new subscription.

<Alert severity="info">
    <strong>Cancelled does not mean gone.</strong> A customer who cancels on day 2 of a monthly subscription
    keeps their access for the remaining 28 days, because they paid for them. The subscription ends when the
    period runs out.
</Alert>

# Renewals

Shortly before each period ends, the provider charges the customer's saved payment method. The store creates
a **pending renewal order**, and completes it when the payment succeeds. The subscription's period end moves
forward only when the money actually arrives, so "renews on" is always a date that has been paid for.

Renewals are quiet by design: **your customers get no email from the store for a successful renewal**. Their
payment provider already sends them a receipt, and monthly "thanks for paying again" mail is how a
subscription ends up cancelled.

Renewals of a manually fulfilled product are the exception — see
[Fulfillment](/plugins/ecommerce-plugin/fulfillment#subscriptions-and-fulfillment).

# When a payment fails

This is the most common thing that happens to a subscription: the card expired.

1. The provider tries to charge and fails. The subscription moves to **Past due**.
2. The customer is emailed, told what happened and given the date their access ends.
3. The provider keeps retrying on its own schedule.
4. If a retry succeeds, the subscription is **Active** again as if nothing had happened.
5. If nothing is paid by the end of the grace period, the subscription is **cancelled and ended**
   automatically, and roles and badges are revoked.

## The grace period

Access is kept until **whichever comes first**:

- the end of the period they did not pay for, or
- **30 days** after the first failed payment.

So a monthly subscriber whose payment fails has up to a month; a yearly subscriber has 30 days, not another
year. The exact date is shown on the customer's subscription page and stated in the email they receive.

<Alert severity="warning">
    This sweep runs on your site's <strong>scheduler</strong>. If background workers are not running, past
    due subscriptions are never ended and customers keep their roles indefinitely. See
    <a href="/plugins/ecommerce-plugin/store-settings#background-workers">Background workers</a>.
</Alert>

# The subscription list

**Admin Panel → Ecommerce → Subscriptions**

![Subscription list](/user-manual/plugins/ecommerce-plugin/subscription-list.png)

Filter by status. The one to watch is **Past due** — those are customers about to lose access who probably
just need to update a card.

The subscription page shows the customer, the product and tier, the price, when it started, when the current
period ends, and **all of its orders** below: the initial purchase and every renewal.

# Cancelling a subscription

## As an admin

On the subscription page, use **Actions → Cancel subscription**.

![Cancel a subscription](/user-manual/plugins/ecommerce-plugin/subscription-cancel.png)

You choose when it ends:

- **At the end of the current period** — the customer keeps access until the period they paid for runs out,
  and is not charged again. This is the kind thing to do for someone who asked to stop.
- **Right away** — the customer loses access immediately. Use this when something has gone wrong.

Neither option refunds anything on its own. If you also want to send the last payment back, tick **Also
refund the last payment** on the same screen — that is the fix for someone who subscribed to the wrong tier.
A refund always ends the subscription right away, even when you picked the end of the period.

The provider is told immediately, so the change takes effect at once rather than waiting for a webhook.

## As a customer

From their subscription page under **My purchases**, customers can cancel themselves. They can only cancel
**at the end of the period** — they paid for it, they keep it, and no money goes back. The page spells that
out before they confirm.

If the product is fulfilled manually, their card then reads "Cancellation requested. An admin is finishing
it." until someone marks it handled. See
[Fulfillment](/plugins/ecommerce-plugin/fulfillment#cancellations-that-need-you).

## Cancelling in the provider's dashboard

You can, and the store will follow along when the provider reports it. But you lose the extras — the
cancellation request, the option to refund in the same action — so the admin panel is the better door.

<Alert severity="info">

    the way to fix a failed payment.
</Alert>

# Changing what a subscriber pays

Existing subscribers are **grandfathered** onto the price they signed up at. Changing a price creates a new
one for new customers and leaves current subscribers where they are.

To move someone to a different amount or a different tier, ask them to cancel and subscribe again. See
[Changing a price](/plugins/ecommerce-plugin/tiers-and-prices#changing-a-price).
