# Tiers &amp; Prices

Prices do not sit on a product directly. A product has **tiers**, and each tier has one or more **prices**.

```
Product  "Supporter"
  ├─ Tier  "Bronze"  ─┬─ Price  €3 / month
  │                   └─ Price  €30 / year
  └─ Tier  "Gold"    ─┬─ Price  €10 / month
                      └─ Price  €100 / year
```

- A **tier** is a variant of the product: Bronze and Gold, Free and Premium, Small and Large.
- A **price** is one way to pay for that tier: monthly, yearly, or once.

Customers pick a tier first, then how they want to be billed. A product with a single tier hides that step
entirely — the buy button goes straight to checkout.

## A single-tier product

If you are just selling one thing at one price — a hoodie, a donation, a plugin license — create one tier
and give it the same label as the product. The storefront notices and puts the buy box straight in the page
header.

## Free tiers

A free tier is a tier with a price of **0**, not a tier without prices. It goes through the whole checkout
(so you still get an order, and the customer still gets their roles and their confirmation), it just never
touches your payment provider.

## One-time and recurring

A **one-time** price is a single payment. The customer buys it, you deliver it, done.

A **recurring** price creates a [subscription](/plugins/ecommerce-plugin/subscriptions). The payment provider
charges the customer every interval and the store records each charge as a new order.

A **free** price is always one-time. There is nothing to renew and no provider to cancel it with, so the
type and interval fields disappear when you set the amount to 0.

## Offering monthly and yearly

The usual setup: one tier, two prices.

1. Create the tier with its monthly price — say €5 per month.
2. On the tier's Prices tab, add a second price: €50 per year.

Customers now see a "Billing" choice on the tier card, and the big price on the card follows whichever
option they pick.

## Deactivating a price

Untick **Active** to stop offering a price without deleting it. It vanishes from the store, and existing
subscriptions on it keep renewing exactly as before.

That is how you retire an option — the old yearly deal, say — without disturbing anyone who is on it.

# Locked prices

Once a price has been **ordered even once**, it is locked. You cannot change its amount, currency, type or
interval any more, and you cannot delete it. The fields show up disabled in the form.

This is deliberate, and every serious payment system works this way: an order points at the price it was
bought at, so if the price could be edited, your order history and your customers' invoices would quietly
change to say something that never happened.

What you **can** still do with a locked price:

- Activate or deactivate it.
- Replace it, with **Change price**.

<Alert severity="info">
    A tier with a locked price cannot be deleted either, and its key can no longer be changed. Locking is
    what keeps sold history honest.
</Alert>

## Changing a price

To raise or lower a locked price, use the **Change price** action on the price row.

![Change price](/user-manual/plugins/ecommerce-plugin/price-change.png)

What happens when you save:

1. A **new price** is created in the same tier, with the amount you entered.
2. The **old price is deactivated** and marked as replaced.
3. The store offers the new price from that moment on.
4. Every existing order keeps pointing at what it was actually sold at.

**Existing subscribers stay on the old price.** They are grandfathered — their subscription keeps billing at
the amount they signed up for, and they are not moved or notified. If you want subscribers to move to the
new amount, you currently have to ask them to cancel and resubscribe.

<Alert severity="warning">
    Because subscribers are grandfathered, a price change is not a price rise for everyone. Plan your prices
    with that in mind: many subscriptions in the wild are the surviving evidence of every price you ever
    offered.
</Alert>

# Deleting

<table>
    <thead>
        <tr>
            <th>You are deleting</th>
            <th>Allowed?</th>
        </tr>
    </thead>
    <tbody>
        <tr>
            <td>A price that was never ordered</td>
            <td>Yes, unless it is the tier's last price</td>
        </tr>
        <tr>
            <td>A price that was ordered</td>
            <td>No. Deactivate it, or use Change price</td>
        </tr>
        <tr>
            <td>A tier's last price</td>
            <td>No. A tier always has at least one price — delete the tier instead</td>
        </tr>
        <tr>
            <td>A tier with prices that were ordered</td>
            <td>No</td>
        </tr>
        <tr>
            <td>A product that was ordered</td>
            <td>No. Archive it instead</td>
        </tr>
    </tbody>
</table>

Everything that refuses to be deleted can be **deactivated or archived**, which gets the same result for your
customers while keeping your records intact.
